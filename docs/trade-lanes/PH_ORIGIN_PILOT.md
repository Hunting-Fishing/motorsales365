# 365 Parts Trade-Lane Pack — Philippines-Origin Pilot

**Parent export plan:** [`../365_PARTS_GLOBAL_EXPORT_DISTRIBUTION_PLAN.md`](../365_PARTS_GLOBAL_EXPORT_DISTRIBUTION_PLAN.md)

**Origin market:** Philippines (`PH`)

**Destination market:** **Not selected — this pack cannot activate a lane until one exact destination is approved**

**Buyer model:** Assisted B2B pilot proposed

**Status:** `research`

**Version:** 0.1

**Updated:** 2026-08-06

> “Philippines to worldwide” is not a trade lane. This document prepares the origin side and defines the destination-selection gate. Activation requires a copied/versioned pack naming one destination customs territory, product class, parties, carrier service, payment route, return route, and warranty route.

## 1. Pilot objective

Prove that 365 can identify demand, source a defensible part from an approved Philippine origin, quote a credible delivered cost, complete export/import documents through qualified parties, deliver to one approved foreign B2B buyer type, reconcile money/customs, and resolve exceptions without accepting unintended legal or financial responsibility.

The first pilot should learn:

- which Philippine-origin or Philippine-stocked parts have foreign demand;
- whether source, fitment, condition, and origin evidence are sufficient;
- broker/carrier document and system requirements;
- true handling, freight, duty/tax, FX, support, return, and warranty cost;
- customs clearance and delivery variability;
- whether the lane can produce positive contribution margin and repeat purchases.

## 2. Proposed narrow scope

### 2.1 Included candidates

- prepaid business buyers such as repair shops, parts stores, restorers, fleets, or approved distributors;
- low-risk, non-dangerous, parcel-sized parts;
- new items from approved, documented sources;
- high-value/low-volume, rare, obsolete, market-specific, or otherwise unavailable demand;
- manual quote and compliance review;
- one origin consolidation/inspection point if justified;
- one licensed/qualified customs broker or filing route and one carrier service;
- one destination-country return/warranty arrangement.

### 2.2 Excluded initially

- consumer mass checkout;
- “ship worldwide” visibility without lane eligibility;
- batteries, airbags/restraints, aerosols, compressed gas, fuels, oils/chemicals, magnetized/radioactive items, and other dangerous goods;
- safety-critical used parts;
- counterfeit-risk or undocumented branded goods;
- emissions defeat, theft/immobilizer, controlled technical/military end-use, or sanctioned/diversion-risk products;
- oversize engines, transmissions, body panels, glass, and freight classes until parcel operations are proven;
- perishables, prohibited wildlife/material content, or uncertain composition;
- DDP or importer-of-record promises without an approved destination model;
- credit funded by 365.

## 4. Destination-selection scorecard

Score candidates with evidence, not personal preference alone.

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Demonstrated demand | RFQs, no-result searches, repair-shop/fleet interviews, historical purchases |
| Product advantage | Rare Philippine/Asian fitment, supplier access, price, lead time, authenticity |
| Catalog/fitment confidence | Destination vehicle population and identity coverage |
| Buyer quality | Verified B2B prospects, repeat potential, payment reliability |
| Customs simplicity | Clear classification/origin/value, low-risk product, broker experience |
| Carrier capability | Accepted product, tracking, service, loss/damage, returns |
| Landed economics | Positive contribution after all handling, support, risk, FX, returns |
| Payment/FX | Supported provider/currencies/refunds and manageable settlement |
| Returns/warranty | Credible destination handling and supplier recovery |
| Legal/product rights | Brand territory, parallel import, product safety, labeling, IP |
| Support | Timezone/language/business-hour coverage |
| Strategic reuse | Enables additional products/buyers without weakening controls |

Decision D-045 records the selected destination. After selection, create a destination-specific file; do not change this generic origin-preparation pack to imply several destinations.

## 5. Parties and responsibility matrix

Before any quote, record RACI for:

- product/source/fitment evidence;
- seller and commercial invoice;
- export registration/declaration and origin documentation;
- classification and customs value inputs;
- packing, labels, pickup, and export handoff;
- screening and permit/license checks;
- freight, insurance, and claims;
- importer, import declaration, duty/tax, and local compliance;
- delivery and proof;
- returns, warranty, recall, and disposal;
- customer support and dispute resolution;
- payment, refund, settlement, tax, and accounting records.

Incoterms do not alone determine title, seller of record, tax, product liability, payment, returns, warranty, or who may legally import/export. Agreements and lane configuration must address them separately.

## 6. Philippine origin readiness

### 6.1 Exporter readiness

Confirm with current Bureau of Customs requirements and the appointed broker/export adviser:

- exporter identity and Client Profile Registration System/other registration as applicable;
- authority and customs account access;
- export declaration preparation/lodgement;
- port/airport process and Authority to Load;
- broker/agent authority;
- record retention, corrections, audits, and post-entry handling;
- product permits, clearances, and agency requirements.

### 6.2 Minimum document workflow

The current BOC export guidance identifies core documents including the export declaration, proforma/commercial invoice, packing list, and other documents as required. The exact lane may also require:

- transport document/air waybill/bill of lading;
- certificate or declaration of origin;
- permits, licenses, certifications, safety data, test or technical evidence;
- insurance and freight evidence;
- buyer purchase order/contract;
- manufacturer/source/brand authorization;
- serial/lot list and dangerous-goods declaration where permitted;
- customs valuation evidence and related-party information;
- destination import documents.

Documents must agree on party names, addresses, product descriptions, quantities, values, currency, classification, origin, weights, packages, and Incoterm/delivery model.

### 6.3 Origin warehouse/consolidation controls

- receive against seller order/transfer and preserve ownership;
- verify part number, brand, condition, quantity, serial/lot, source, and fitment evidence;
- inspect damage/tampering and quarantine discrepancies;
- consolidate only compatible products and customer orders;
- use export-appropriate packaging, labels, weights, and dimensions;
- capture packing photos and custody events;
- prevent shipment before lane/compliance/document approval;
- reconcile handoff, carrier acceptance, and export clearance.

## 7. Product eligibility decision

Each product/order passes:

1. canonical identity and seller-item mapping;
2. defensible customer application/fitment or explicit non-fitment universal use;
3. approved source, brand territory, condition, and authenticity evidence;
4. origin-market sale/export eligibility;
5. destination import/sale/use eligibility and responsible-party evidence;
6. classification, origin, value, permit, sanctions/export-control screening;
7. carrier/service acceptance and packaging;
8. return/warranty route and product-liability decision;
9. positive or explicitly approved experimental unit economics;
10. complete data/documents before release.

Output states:

- `eligible`;
- `eligible-manual-review`;
- `eligible-with-conditions`;
- `ineligible-temporary`;
- `ineligible-prohibited`;
- `insufficient-evidence`.

## 8. Customer/B2B journey

### 8.1 RFQ

Buyer supplies:

- verified business identity and contacts;
- destination and importer details;
- vehicle/equipment identity and application;
- requested part number, photos/specifications, quantity, condition, urgency;
- substitution acceptance boundaries;
- delivery location/use/end user information required for screening;
- tax/resale/import documents where applicable.

### 8.2 Source and fitment review

365/partner records candidate products, evidence, uncertainties, source, condition, lead time, and why each offer is eligible.

### 8.3 Quote

Show separately:

- item and origin handling;
- international freight and insurance;
- brokerage/disbursement;
- estimated or guaranteed duty/tax as contractually supported;
- destination delivery;
- 365 fees/markup;
- currency/FX rate and expiry;
- importer, duty payer, and clearance responsibilities;
- delivery estimate and non-guaranteed events;
- fitment/condition, return, warranty, cancellation, and customs-adjustment terms.

### 8.4 Acceptance and payment

- verified authorized buyer accepts policy/quote version;
- payment method and fraud/compliance checks pass;
- stock and quote are revalidated;
- seller, exporter, importer, broker, carrier, return, and warranty parties freeze;
- order/settlement/doc tasks are created atomically or compensated safely.

### 8.5 Tracking and clearance

Customer sees operational events without exposing private broker/security data:

`confirmed → sourcing/reserved → origin inspection → packed → export documents → carrier accepted → export cleared/departed → destination customs → out for delivery → delivered → closed/exception`

### 8.6 Post-delivery

- proof and condition acknowledgement;
- installation/outcome where buyer participates;
- return/warranty/shortage/damage reporting;
- customs adjustment and final financial reconciliation;
- buyer/supplier/route performance review.

## 9. Landed-cost controls

### 9.1 Components

- product price and origin taxes where applicable;
- domestic origin transport;
- consolidation, inspection, documentation, export packing;
- export broker/filing/port fees;
- international freight, fuel/remote/security surcharges;
- insurance;
- destination brokerage/disbursement/handling;
- duty, VAT/GST/sales/other taxes and trade remedies;
- destination delivery;
- payment/FX costs;
- 365 fees/markup;
- contingency allowed by policy.

### 9.2 Quote version

Store:

- source price and stock timestamp;
- classification/origin/value version;
- carrier service/rate and weight/dimensions;
- tax/duty method;
- currencies, FX source/rate/time;
- expiration and re-quote triggers;
- who bears variance;
- refund FX rule;
- assumptions excluded from the quote.

### 9.3 Adjustment handling

No silent post-payment charge. The approved model must define customer authorization, 365/seller absorption, cancellation, abandonment, dispute, and customs correction for differences.

## 10. Classification, origin, and value

- Use product-specific tariff classification with evidence and broker/customs review appropriate to risk.
- Manufacturing origin is not the warehouse/export country.
- Preferential origin requires qualifying rules and evidence; do not claim based only on shipping country.
- Customs value must address invoice price, assists, royalties, related parties, discounts, freight/insurance, free replacements, samples, and non-sale movements as applicable.
- Warranty replacements, returns, cores, samples, and free goods still require controlled values and customs treatment.
- Binding rulings/advance decisions should be considered for repeat, material, or uncertain classifications/origin/valuation.

## 11. Screening and prohibited activity

Screen at onboarding, quote, pre-payment, pre-shipment, and material-change points as required:

- buyer, seller, exporter, importer, consignee, end user, payment parties, beneficial/control parties;
- destination, transshipment, end use, product technical characteristics;
- sanctions, denied/restricted parties, export controls, diversion, military/dual-use risk;
- fraud, stolen payment, account takeover, identity inconsistency;
- counterfeit, unauthorized territory, brand/IP, and parallel-import restrictions;
- customs undervaluation, false description, split shipment, false gift/sample, or origin manipulation.

Matches or uncertainty create holds for qualified review; the system must not auto-clear based only on fuzzy-name scoring.

## 12. Logistics, packaging, and custody

- carrier acceptance confirmed for exact product/service/destination;
- accurate package count, weights, dimensions, commodity description, value, and labels;
- category-specific packaging and tamper evidence;
- no unapproved dangerous goods or incompatible consolidation;
- scanned custody at origin partner, consolidator, carrier, customs/exception, destination, and delivery;
- high-value signature and insurance where approved;
- loss/damage evidence and claim deadlines;
- carrier/provider outage and route-change controls;
- no unauthorized transshipment or address redirection.

## 13. Returns, warranties, and reverse customs

Before activation define:

- destination reporting window and evidence;
- local assessment versus return-to-origin;
- wrong item, fitment error, defect, transit damage, incomplete, customs refusal, buyer refusal, and change-of-mind treatment;
- return authorization and carrier;
- importer/exporter for the reverse movement;
- declared value, reason-for-return, duty/tax recovery, temporary import/re-export where applicable;
- replacement shipment and valuation;
- supplier warranty recovery;
- abandonment, disposal, salvage, or donation authority;
- customer refund timing and deductions;
- cross-border core eligibility/deposit/return.

The lane does not activate for consumer volume if the expected return cost makes the customer remedy unrealistic.

## 14. Finance and settlement

Create immutable entries for:

- buyer collection;
- seller proceeds;
- 365 sourcing/marketplace/logistics revenue;
- origin handling and carrier/broker payables;
- duty/tax and customs advances;
- payment and FX;
- partner payout hold/release;
- refund, warranty, chargeback, and recovery;
- customs/FX adjustment;
- intercompany entries if 365 entities participate.

Daily during the pilot, reconcile order, provider, bank, carrier/broker, seller, tax/duty, and reserve records.

## 15. Systems and Supabase configuration

Minimum linked records:

- `trade_lane` and immutable `trade_lane_version`;
- country, product risk, classification, origin, and compliance rules;
- exporter/importer/broker/carrier/provider approvals;
- RFQ, quote, quote components, assumptions, and acceptance;
- cross-border order responsibility snapshot;
- export/import document tasks and evidence;
- shipment packages, custody, customs, and exceptions;
- landed-cost estimate/final reconciliation;
- screening decisions and protected evidence;
- return/warranty/reverse-customs case;
- lane performance and suspension events.

RLS must separate buyer, seller, broker, carrier, internal compliance, finance, and support views. Sensitive screening/customs identity records are not general partner/customer data.

## 16. Pilot limits

The destination-specific activation must set numeric limits for:

- sellers, origin locations, and buyers;
- product classes/SKUs and quantities;
- shipment/order value and monthly exposure;
- packages, weight, dimensions, and service;
- manual reviewer workload;
- open returns/warranties/customs exceptions;
- payout hold and reserves;
- maximum approved learning subsidy/loss.

Limits are enforced by the order engine, not kept only in this document.

## 17. Acceptance tests

### Identity and eligibility

- exact parties and roles freeze per order;
- ineligible product/destination/service combinations cannot quote or ship;
- expired supplier, lane, carrier, permit, or rule approval blocks release;
- uncertain fitment/source/origin produces a hold.

### Quote and finance

- landed-cost components and currencies reconcile;
- quote expiry/revalidation works;
- refund and FX/customs adjustment paths reconcile;
- payout cannot release before the configured event;
- duplicate webhooks/jobs do not duplicate money or shipments.

### Documents and logistics

- invoice, packing list, declaration inputs, airway bill, origin/permit evidence agree;
- package weight/dimension/description validation works;
- broker/carrier rejection creates recoverable states;
- custody, loss, damage, customs hold, return-to-sender, and abandonment are handled.

### Returns and support

- wrong item, fitment error, damage, defect, customs refusal, and buyer refusal each reach the correct responsible party;
- reverse customs and replacement values are recorded;
- customer communication, escalation, and evidence are complete.

### Security/compliance

- tenant and role isolation passes;
- screening data has restricted access;
- audit events cannot be altered by normal users;
- suspension blocks new quotes/shipments while preserving support for open orders.

## 18. Pilot KPIs

- RFQ-to-qualified-quote and quote-to-order conversion;
- source/stock/fitment confirmation time;
- quote-to-final landed-cost variance;
- document defect and customs query/hold rate;
- export clearance, transit, import clearance, and delivery time;
- correct item/condition/quantity and damage/loss;
- return, warranty, dispute, and support minutes/order;
- contribution margin and cash days/order;
- repeat B2B buyer rate;
- supplier, broker, carrier, and destination service performance.

## 19. Stop conditions

Suspend new quotes or shipments for:

- regulatory, sanctions, export-control, permit, product-safety, or carrier ineligibility;
- unclear exporter/importer or customs responsibility;
- credible counterfeit/source/brand-territory concern;
- repeated classification, value, origin, or document errors;
- material customs seizures/penalties or unexplained holds;
- payment/FX/payout/reconciliation failure;
- loss, damage, return, warranty, support, or landed-cost variance above approved limits;
- inability to provide credible customer remedy;
- data/security incident or audit failure;
- reserve/cash coverage below the lane floor.

Open orders receive managed resolution; suspension does not abandon customers or statutory obligations.
