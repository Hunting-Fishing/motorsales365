# 365 Vehicle History and Roadworthiness Network

## Complete Outreach, Partnership and Pilot Playbook

**Public product:** 365 Verified Vehicle History  
**Partner program:** 365 Vehicle History and Roadworthiness Network  
**Launch market:** Philippines  
**Expansion direction:** Asia-Pacific, Europe and North America through separate country adapters  
**Status:** Pre-partnership; no government or insurer endorsement or data access should be implied  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-06

> Know what happened. Know what was installed. Know who verified it.

## 1. Executive decision

365 should build a Philippine-first vehicle lifecycle network rather than a simple CARFAX copy. The core product should combine verified vehicle identity observations, repair orders, inspections, diagnostic scans, odometer readings, installed-part lifecycles, warranty events and evidence. Government, law-enforcement, insurer, inspection, dealer and origin-market data become separately licensed trust layers.

The business can launch before LTO integration because participating shops and suppliers can create useful records immediately. Government access is a later controlled verification capability, not a dependency and not a public-registry copy.

The strongest differentiator is component-level history:

- a part ordered is not treated as installed;
- installation records preserve manufacturer, part number, supplier, condition, location, technician, repair order, date, mileage/hours, warranty and evidence;
- removal, failure, comeback, warranty claim, core return and replacement remain linked to the same part or component instance; and
- engines, transmissions, batteries, ECUs, odometer clusters, truck components and motorcycle engines retain their own identity and lifecycle.

365 should not claim to be the first service to record parts or the only Philippine history provider. The defensible public claim is that 365 records deeper, source-labelled vehicle, repair and installed-component history when that data is available.

## 2. What 365 is - and is not

### 2.1 What it is

- An evidence and provenance network attached to Shop Manager, Parts Network, Scan Companion, Marketplace and Export.
- A report showing facts supplied by identified sources and the limits of the search.
- A private owner vehicle vault plus a separately redacted buyer report.
- A contributor system for verified shops, insurers, inspectors, dealers, suppliers and approved authorities.
- A continuing history that can make future selling, purchasing, warranty and repair decisions safer.

### 2.2 What it is not

- Not proof that an unreported event never happened.
- Not a substitute for a current pre-purchase inspection, test drive, document review or official transfer procedure.
- Not an owner database, people-search product or vehicle-location tracker.
- Not a system for publishing allegations, investigative details, customer complaints, shop margins or previous-owner identity.
- Not permission to scrape government, insurer, OEM, auction or subscription portals.
- Not a guarantee of current mechanical condition.

### 2.3 CARFAX benchmark translated into 365 rules

365 can learn from public-facing CARFAX practices without claiming access to CARFAX internal systems:

1. Aggregate many independent sources, but identify what was actually searched.
2. State that reports may be incomplete and recommend inspection and test drive.
3. Preserve a formal correction channel with supporting-document review.
4. Flag mileage inconsistencies rather than declaring fraud from a weak signal.
5. Offer any future guarantee only for narrow, contractually defined, authoritative fields and only after insurance and legal review.
6. Keep seller, buyer, owner and service-shop products connected to the same history record.

365 should not launch a broad buyback promise during the MVP. A later `365 Verified Status Protection` product could cover a narrow missed official status only if a partner feed, underwriting, reserves, eligibility rules and claims operations make it supportable.

## 3. Partner value proposition

### 3.1 LTO

365 asks for a controlled, purpose-bound vehicle status verification - not a registry dump. The public-interest case is safer sales, stronger identifier matching, improved ownership transfer, odometer evidence, recall completion and traceable major component changes.

365 offers:

- consented and source-labelled shop observations;
- odometer and identity evidence captured during real service events;
- repair, inspection and installed-component traceability;
- a controlled referral path for conflicts; and
- de-identified roadworthiness and fleet-quality analysis, subject to approval.

### 3.2 PNP Highway Patrol Group

365 asks whether a submitted identity requires official review through an authorized status or referral workflow. It does not request investigative notes or allow public criminal allegations.

365 offers:

- anti-enumeration controls;
- preserved query purpose and evidence;
- qualified pre-sale verification referrals; and
- a rapid stop-and-escalate workflow for identity conflicts.

### 3.3 Insurance Commission and insurers

365 proposes a common, privacy-minimized motor-loss event standard: loss category, event date/range, material damage classification, total-loss/restoration state, theft/recovery state and approved repair completion - without policyholder identity, private claim files or unsupported fault conclusions.

365 offers repair orders, part instances, inspection evidence and diagnostic pre/post results that can improve claims traceability, warranty handling and fraud controls.

### 3.4 Inspection centres

PMVIC/PETC and independent inspection partners can publish signed identity, date, reading, result and advisory events. Official regulatory inspections remain visually and technically separate from 365 pre-purchase inspections.

### 3.5 OEMs, dealers and importers

The proposed feed includes approved warranty service, recall/campaign completion, mileage, genuine-part installation and major component replacement. 365 offers owner retention, recall leads, parts traceability and dealer attribution.

### 3.6 Repair shops and technicians

The shop performs its normal RO workflow. 365 converts approved close-out facts into a verified history event. The shop receives a contributor badge, easier warranty/comeback evidence, customer retention tools, report credits and a stronger resale story for serviced vehicles.

No contribution reward may depend on hiding negative events or reporting only successful outcomes.

### 3.7 Parts distributors and retailers

Suppliers contribute structured purchase, item, serial/lot, shipment, return and warranty facts. The installation event remains controlled by the RO and technician confirmation.

365 offers an order channel, fitment feedback, warranty traceability and evidence that a genuine or approved part reached the intended vehicle.

### 3.8 Banks, lenders, fleets, auctions and exporters

Subject to lawful basis and contracts, partners can use purpose-limited identity, condition, maintenance, encumbrance, custody and export history. Report access never becomes permission to locate or profile individuals.

## 7. Exact LTO request

### 7.1 Requested capability

365 submits normalized vehicle identifiers and a purpose-bound transaction token. LTO-controlled infrastructure may respond with an approved status result such as:

- exact identifier match / mismatch / unable to verify;
- active/inactive/unknown registration status if permitted;
- transfer or change process status if permitted;
- identity or documentation review required;
- last verified transaction or inspection date at an approved level of precision;
- approved non-personal vehicle descriptors; and
- source timestamp and response reference.

The first request excludes owner names, addresses, IDs, photographs, signatures, contact details, full OR/CR images, payment information and unrestricted historical registry records.

### 7.2 Preferred technical pattern

1. Purpose-bound user obtains authorization and enters/scans identifiers.
2. 365 normalizes values and sends a server-to-server signed request.
3. Partner middleware authenticates 365, validates purpose, rate and scope.
4. Partner returns a minimal response with reference and permitted expiry.
5. Raw restricted responses stay in a segregated partner zone only as long as contractually allowed.
6. The consumer report receives only approved status text and source/freshness metadata.
7. Every access and report display is auditable.

Partner-controlled redirect or one-time verification tokens are acceptable alternatives when direct response data is not permitted.

### 7.3 Government pilot phases

- Phase A: 365-only shop history and synthetic LTO responses.
- Phase B: LTO sandbox using synthetic or masked test identities.
- Phase C: live status verification for allowlisted pilot shops and owner-authorized buyer reports.
- Phase D: evaluated expansion only after joint privacy, security, accuracy and public-service review.

## 8. Buyer and seller safety design

### 8.1 Buyer journey

1. Preview which sources and countries are available before payment.
2. Scan the physical VIN/chassis/frame and compare it to the listing and seller documents.
3. Purchase a timestamped report with report ID, hash and coverage table.
4. Review identity conflicts, mileage timeline, loss/damage, inspection, service, installed components, recalls and unresolved disputes.
5. Obtain a current independent inspection and diagnostic pre-scan.
6. Verify seller authority and follow official transfer/HPG/LTO procedures.
7. Pay through a safe, documented method; any escrow must be provided by a licensed/contracted service.
8. Store the transaction packet and receive future corrections to the purchased snapshot where policy permits.

### 8.2 Seller journey

1. Claim the vehicle with tiered evidence and identity review.
2. Confirm identifiers and disclose known material facts.
3. Review private records and choose which eligible documents to share.
4. Resolve inaccurate events through the formal dispute process; accurate negative events cannot be purchased away.
5. Obtain a current inspection and attach a fresh report to the listing.
6. Give the buyer a time-limited share or report ID rather than sending unredacted OR/CR and IDs through chat.
7. Complete official transfer and revoke temporary shares.

### 8.3 Safety labels

Every report must visibly distinguish:

- verified event;
- source-reported event;
- owner-submitted evidence;
- unverified assertion;
- inferred risk indicator;
- disputed/pending review;
- corrected/superseded; and
- source not searched or unavailable.

Avoid a single "vehicle score" during the MVP. A compressed score can hide material facts and create disputes. Use clear findings, evidence tiers and coverage instead.

## 9. Data and evidence rules

### 9.1 Asset identity

The system supports VIN, chassis, frame, engine, plate, MV file, conduction sticker, OR/CR references, manufacturer codes, trailer/body identifiers, auction/import references and component serials. It cannot force all Asian or motorcycle records into a 17-character VIN.

Identifiers are assertions from sources, not blindly overwritten master values. Conflicts create review cases and block automatic report merging.

### 9.2 Append-only history

At the audit layer, history events are append-only. A correction links a new decision to the original event. Public display may suppress or replace a disputed assertion, but the evidence and adjudication trail remain restricted when lawfully retained.

Privacy rights may still require deletion, blocking, restriction or pseudonymization. Append-only integrity is never used to avoid legal privacy obligations.

### 9.3 Event minimums

Every publishable event includes:

- asset and relevant component;
- event and observation time;
- source organization/system/record;
- source country and jurisdiction;
- facts asserted in structured fields;
- evidence reference and hash;
- verification tier;
- consent/lawful basis and purpose;
- visibility and retention policy;
- contributor/reviewer;
- correction/supersession links; and
- published/report snapshot timestamps.

### 9.4 Installed parts

The lifecycle states are ordered, received, allocated, installed, verified, removed, returned/core returned, warranty claim, failed and superseded/replaced. Only a completed installation confirmation linked to an RO can publish an installed-part event.

Safety- or identity-critical components require stronger evidence, for example technician identity, label/serial photo, install position, post-repair inspection/scan and legal document status where required.

## 10. Privacy and security operating model

### 10.1 Data separation

Use three zones:

1. **Private operations zone** - customers, owner claims, ROs, invoices, shop notes and source documents.
2. **Restricted partner zone** - government/insurer responses, credentials, contract-bound fields and raw payloads.
3. **Public report zone** - redacted facts, source labels, freshness, coverage and report snapshots.

The public report service must not have a direct path to previous-owner PII or unrestricted raw partner data.

### 10.2 Required controls

- Zero-trust server-side authorization and least privilege.
- MFA/passkeys and just-in-time privileged access.
- Tenant, role, purpose, source, field, country and report entitlement checks.
- HMAC lookup tokens plus encryption for high-risk identifiers; masked display by default.
- TLS for all transport; mTLS/private-key client authentication for sensitive partner APIs where supported.
- Central key/secret management and scheduled rotation.
- Signed/idempotent partner submissions with replay protection.
- Isolated evidence storage, malware scanning and short-lived signed access.
- Append-only security/audit logs, event hashes and periodic integrity verification.
- Rate limits, bot detection, query budgets and bulk-export denial.
- Report watermarks, viewer identity, expiry and QR/hash verification.
- Secure SDLC, code review, dependency scanning, backup/restore tests and penetration testing.
- Vendor due diligence and subprocessor inventory.

### 10.3 Abuse cases to design against

- VIN/plate enumeration and owner discovery.
- Vehicle stalking through recent service locations.
- Insider searches for celebrities, rivals or repossession targets.
- Seller attempts to hide accurate negative events.
- Shop fabrication of repairs or parts installation.
- Supplier invoice reuse across vehicles.
- Duplicate/cloned identities and deliberate near-match identifiers.
- Credential stuffing, shared shop accounts and malicious API clients.
- Report alteration, screenshot reuse and expired report resale.
- AI extraction errors creating false events.

Recent locations and exact timestamps should be hidden or generalized on consumer reports unless necessary and authorized. High-risk queries require a recorded purpose and may require owner authorization.

## 11. Dispute and correction program

### 11.1 Intake

Accept disputes from owners, sellers, buyers, shops and source partners. Require the report/event reference, disputed field, requested correction, relationship to the vehicle and supporting evidence. Do not require more personal data than necessary.

### 11.2 Triage

- Urgent identity/theft/wrong-vehicle issue: immediate suppression or prominent dispute label; review within one business day.
- Material loss, mileage, ownership or component issue: target initial review within two business days.
- Routine service-description correction: target initial review within five business days.

These are pilot service targets, not statutory promises, and must be staffed before publication.

### 11.3 Investigation

1. Freeze the report snapshot and preserve evidence.
2. Verify the claimant's standing without exposing previous-owner data.
3. Contact the source through the agreed correction channel.
4. Compare source, documents, identity and adjacent events.
5. Record reviewer, reasoning and evidence.
6. Confirm, correct, supersede, remove from public display or leave disputed with explanation.
7. Notify affected report purchasers where policy and law permit.
8. Feed root causes into contributor and matching controls.

365 must never reveal confidential source details merely to defend a report. The customer receives enough reasoning and remedy information without receiving another person's personal data or protected investigation material.

## 12. Incident and breach response

### 12.1 Minimum response sequence

1. Detect and open a timestamped incident record.
2. Classify severity and personal-data involvement.
3. Contain credentials, sessions, integrations or data paths.
4. Preserve evidence and maintain chain of custody.
5. Assess scope, affected sources, data subjects and likely harm.
6. Engage the DPO, incident commander, counsel and affected partner contacts.
7. Decide and execute NPC/data-subject/partner notification within applicable deadlines; the Philippine workflow must be capable of meeting the 72-hour requirement when notification is mandatory.
8. Eradicate cause, rotate credentials and restore clean service.
9. Validate integrity and monitor recurrence.
10. Complete post-incident report, corrective actions and annual incident reporting.

### 12.2 Pilot stop conditions

Pause affected live access when any of the following occurs:

- unauthorized cross-tenant or previous-owner disclosure;
- uncontained partner credential compromise;
- evidence of systematic false identity merges;
- bulk enumeration or scraping that controls cannot stop;
- report content exceeds signed field/display rights;
- material source corruption or untraceable events;
- inability to meet incident or dispute obligations; or
- direction from an authorized partner/regulator.

## 13. Agreement checklist

Every DSA/MOA/licence/API agreement must identify:

- legal parties and authority to share;
- controller/PIC and processor/PIP roles by processing activity;
- exact purposes and prohibited purposes;
- exact data fields, direction and source ownership;
- users, roles, territories, vehicle classes and volumes;
- lawful basis/consent and transparency obligations;
- display, caching, derived-data, analytics, AI, print/export and resale rights;
- identity matching, no-result, correction and withdrawal rules;
- security controls, authentication, testing and audit rights;
- subprocessors, hosting regions and cross-border transfers;
- incident contacts and notification timing;
- retention, deletion/return and termination handling;
- SLAs, support, planned changes and data freshness;
- fees, tax, minimums, reconciliation and overage;
- attribution, trademarks and public-announcement approval;
- liability, indemnity, insurance and limitation of liability;
- suspension, dispute, governing law and termination; and
- exit assistance and post-termination report obligations.

No agreement should give a partner ownership of 365-created shop events, installed-part lifecycles, software, aggregate learnings or unrelated customer relationships unless intentionally negotiated.

## 15. Pilot acceptance measures

Key measures are also provided in the scorecard CSV.

### Safety and identity

- Zero automatic merges where critical identifiers conflict.
- At least 99.5% exact attachment of tested events to the intended pilot asset, with all remaining cases queued for review.
- 100% of reports show source coverage, freshness and limitations.
- 100% of installed-part events trace to an RO and installer confirmation.

### Privacy and security

- Zero unauthorized previous-owner PII in consumer reports.
- Zero successful bulk enumeration in acceptance testing.
- 100% MFA coverage for privileged users.
- 100% partner queries carry user, purpose, scope and audit context.
- Backup restoration and incident tabletop pass before live partner data.

### Operations

- 95% of valid event submissions accepted or clearly rejected within the target processing window.
- Urgent wrong-vehicle reports triaged within one business day.
- Source corrections propagate to new reports within the contractual window.

### Public value and adoption

- Buyer comprehension test shows users understand that no record is not proof of no event.
- Measured increase in sellers completing disclosures and buyers obtaining inspections.
- Evidence of improved parts/warranty traceability and transfer readiness.

## 16. Commercial safeguards

- Evidence grade, search result and dispute outcome are never influenced by payment.
- Ads and affiliate parts offers stay visually separated from history findings.
- A shop cannot pay to remove accurate negative events.
- Contributors are rewarded for completeness and quality, not positive-only histories.
- Refund and report-update policy is clear before purchase.
- Report pricing identifies optional official/licensed source costs.
- Enterprise use is purpose-bound and cannot become consumer surveillance.
