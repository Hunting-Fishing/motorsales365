# 365 Vehicle History Network - Partner RFI and Due Diligence

Complete this for every government, insurer, inspection, OEM, parts, diagnostic, auction, lender, fleet or origin-history source. Marketing materials and verbal assurances are not sufficient evidence.

## A. Organization and authority

1. Full legal name, registration/jurisdiction and address.
2. Contracting entity and authorized signatory.
3. Product/data owner and operational contact.
4. DPO/privacy, security and incident contacts.
5. What statute, mandate, consent, contract or licence authorizes collection and sharing?
6. Who owns/controls each proposed field and document?
7. Is another authority, OEM, insurer, customer or data subject approval required?
8. Are there restrictions on commercial consumer reports, dealer use, lender use, insurer use, export use or analytics?
9. Are any fields investigatory, confidential, privileged, copyrighted or trade-secret protected?
10. What attribution, seal, trademark and public-announcement rules apply?

## B. Data roles and privacy

11. Which party is PIC/controller, joint PIC/controller or PIP/processor for each activity?
12. What lawful basis is expected for query, ingestion, report display, retention and correction?
13. When is owner/driver/customer consent required?
14. What notice must be presented and by whom?
15. What data-subject request channel applies?
16. How will access, correction, objection, erasure/blocking and complaint requests be coordinated?
17. Is cross-border storage/support allowed? In which countries/regions?
18. Which subprocessors may receive the data?
19. Is automated extraction, risk detection, AI processing, indexing or translation allowed?
20. Is de-identified/aggregate analytics allowed and under what threshold/re-identification controls?

## C. Coverage and identity

21. Countries, regions, vehicle classes, model years and earliest event date.
22. VIN, chassis, frame, engine, plate, MV file and other supported identifiers.
23. Handling of non-17-character VINs, JDM chassis codes, motorcycles, trucks and changed engines/components.
24. Match method and exact/possible/conflict response states.
25. Known duplicate, clone, reissue and identifier-change conditions.
26. Expected exact-match, no-result, false-positive and false-merge rates.
27. Coverage by event type and source organization.
28. Events known to be excluded or delayed.
29. How is source-market versus Philippine-market identity distinguished?
30. Can an identity be tested in sandbox with synthetic cases for matches, conflicts and near matches?

## D. Fields and rights

31. Exact field name, type, definition, units and allowed values.
32. Whether each field may be queried, received, cached, displayed, printed, exported or resold.
33. Whether full facts, status codes, documents, thumbnails, metadata or links are permitted.
34. Required masking or aggregation.
35. Public, owner-only, shop-only, enterprise-only or authority-only use.
36. Permitted retention and refresh period.
37. Required deletion/return at termination.
38. Whether historical report snapshots may be retained after source expiry/correction.
39. Rights to derived risk indicators and combined 365 events.
40. Rights to audit evidence and source response references.

## E. Accuracy, corrections and freshness

41. Update frequency and expected delay from real-world event to feed.
42. Source timestamp, ingestion timestamp and effective date availability.
43. Push, polling or re-query model.
44. Correction, withdrawal and supersession mechanism.
45. Partner correction contact and target turnaround.
46. Whether previous report purchasers may be notified.
47. How no-result, timeout, unavailable and unsupported differ.
48. Data-quality reports and source-level error metrics available.
49. How source schema/value changes are announced.
50. Whether a report may show the source organization and response time.

## F. Technical integration

51. API, batch, embedded viewer, partner redirect or report-validation options.
52. Sandbox, test accounts and synthetic data.
53. Authentication: OAuth, private key, mTLS, IP allowlist, VPN or other.
54. Signing, nonce, timestamp, replay and idempotency support.
55. API schemas, versioning and deprecation notice.
56. Rate limits, daily/monthly quotas and concurrency limits.
57. Service levels, maintenance windows and status page.
58. Error codes and retry requirements.
59. Webhook security and delivery/replay behavior.
60. Data residency, hosting and backup regions.

## G. Security assurance

61. Security program owner and current independent certifications/assessments.
62. Encryption and key-management controls.
63. MFA, privileged access and access-review controls.
64. Logging, monitoring and audit-retention controls.
65. Vulnerability, patch, dependency and penetration-test program.
66. Incident response and maximum time to notify 365.
67. Business continuity, disaster recovery, tested RTO/RPO.
68. Subprocessor and fourth-party risk management.
69. Secure deletion and media disposal.
70. Evidence that access is limited to authorized purpose/users.

## H. Commercial and legal

71. Pilot, setup, minimum, subscription, per-query, overage and support fees.
72. Taxes, currency, invoicing and payment terms.
73. Term, renewal, minimum commitment and termination.
74. Territory, vehicle class, channel and exclusivity.
75. Liability, indemnity, insurance and warranty positions.
76. Data-quality/service remedies and credits.
77. Audit and compliance-assessment rights.
78. Public reference/customer/logo permission.
79. Exit assistance, credentials, data return/deletion and surviving report obligations.
80. Procurement, accreditation or government approval steps and expected timeline.

## I. Pilot proof required

- Signed field/right matrix.
- Sandbox and test results.
- At least 25 exact pilot vehicles per applicable domain/region.
- Match/conflict/no-result cases.
- Correction and withdrawal test.
- Rate-limit and abuse test.
- Security questionnaire and evidence review.
- PIA/DSA/MOA/licence completion.
- Joint incident tabletop.
- Written live-pilot approval.

## J. Automatic disqualification or hold

- Cannot prove authority to share or commercial display rights.
- Requires scraping or credential sharing.
- Prohibits required correction/data-subject remedies.
- Cannot separate no-result from clean/no-event.
- Requires silent cross-market or family-level matching.
- Demands publication of personal or investigative data unnecessary for purpose.
- Cannot support basic access control, encryption, incident or deletion requirements.
- Claims universal coverage without exact test results.
- Requires 365 to hide source limitations or sell positive-only results.
